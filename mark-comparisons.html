Mark Comparisons

Phase: Discover Weight: 5% Due: Monday, August 31

Ten logos, five business types, one page. This is the first assignment of the semester, and the "bad" marks you pick here become the working pool you'll draw from for everything else you build this semester.

The Assignment

  • 10 comparisons across 5 business types.Manufacturing, retail, professional, service, and non-profit. For each type, choose one good mark and one bad mark.
  • Bad marks must be local.Akron, Cleveland, Columbus, or Pittsburgh. A real business you could plausibly work with.
  • Good marks can come from anywhere.National or international, any brand you think is genuinely well designed.
  • Format.One clean, consistent page (17×11) with a short written rationale for every mark explaining why it's good or why it's bad.
  • This is about taste, not right or wrong.There's no correct answer here, just your ability to see design quality and explain your thinking. Taste like this is a foundational skill for a designer. Say why each mark works or doesn't, and back it up.

What's Next

  • Present to the group and discuss.Be ready to explain why each mark is good or bad.
  • There's more to come.We'll keep working with the bad marks throughout the Discover phase. One of your five bad marks moves forward and becomes the business you'll design for the rest of the semester, so don't pick one you wouldn't want to spend it on.
  • Duplicates get sorted and the pool narrows.If you and a classmate picked the same local bad mark, that's resolved as part of narrowing down in the next part of the phase. Once that's sorted, each student ends up with a different business. That's the one you'll rebrand for the rest of the semester.

Examples

Real, strong examples from past students. Meant to show what a finished page can look like, not templates to copy.
