Brand Guide + Applications

Phase: Develop + Deliver Weight: 60% Due: Wednesday, December 9

Your system goes to work. You’ll design the pieces your business would actually use, then document the whole brand in a guide someone else could pick up and follow.

Getting Started

  • One direction.Everything here is built on the one brand and concept you locked in on October 19.
  • Start with the website.
    • It tests your whole system at once: type hierarchy, color, imagery, grid.
    • Get it working before you spread out to everything else.
  • Get my OK on your business-specific applications (see ideas below).
    • Due Monday, October 26.
    • Show me in class, or email jkurtz@uakron.edu.
    • For each one: what it is, and why your business would need it.

The Applications

  • Website.
    • Home page and one interior page.
    • Each at a desktop and a mobile size, so four layouts.
    • Pick one desktop and one mobile width (in pixels; height is up to you, pages scroll):
      • Desktop: 1440 (most common), 1920, 1366, or 1280
      • Mobile: 390 (current iPhone), 393, 375, 360, or 412
    • Check your text contrast with the WebAIM Contrast Checker.
  • Business card.
    • Front and back.
    • Standard 3.5 × 2 in.
  • Presentation template.
    • A deck template system, 16:9.
    • Three slide types: title, chapter, and interior.
    • You’re designing a system, not one presentation, but fill it with real content for your brand.
  • Campaign.
    • One campaign idea: a single message or promotion your business would actually run (a seasonal sale, a grand reopening, a fundraiser, a new service).
    • That one idea shows up in all of these:
      • Two print ads (poster, print ad, or billboard)
      • The same two ads, adapted as social posts
      • A social profile image and banner, on one platform that fits your business
      • An email announcing the campaign
      • A landing page the ads and email point to (desktop only)
  • Three business-specific applications.
    • Things your business would actually need.
    • Use the ideas list below. Pick from any category, or propose something that’s not listed.
    • Each one needs my OK (see Getting Started).

For Every Application

  • Real copy, no placeholder text.
    • Every headline, line of body copy, and button is real and written for your brand.
    • Writing it yourself is best.
    • AI is allowed for copy only. Come up with the ideas yourself first, especially your campaign idea, then AI can help you flesh them out.
    • If you use AI, say so: note which pieces in your Process Book.
    • However it’s written, it has to be good.
    • Logos, imagery, and layouts can’t be AI-generated (see the AI policy in the syllabus).
  • Show everything in context.
    • Every application in a realistic mockup.
    • Don’t reuse the same mockup twice.

Business-Specific Ideas

Every type of business needs different things. These are examples, grouped by the five business types from Mark Comparisons, not a complete list.

  • Manufacturing.
    • Trade show booth
    • Product labels or packaging
    • Sell sheet or product spec sheet
    • Capabilities brochure
    • Fleet vehicle graphics
    • Uniforms or safety gear
    • Facility signage and wayfinding
    • Catalog spread
  • Retail.
    • Storefront signage or window graphics
    • Shopping bags and packaging
    • Hang tags, price tags, and shelf talkers
    • In-store displays
    • Gift card or loyalty card
    • Merch (tee, tote, stickers)
    • Staff apparel or name tags
    • E-commerce product page
  • Professional.
    • Office signage and wayfinding
    • Lobby or environmental graphics
    • Welcome or onboarding kit
    • Brochure or service guide
    • Presentation folder
    • Report cover and spread
    • Seminar or event materials
  • Service.Restaurants, pet care, home services, venues, recreation.
    • Vehicle graphics
    • Uniforms or staff apparel
    • Menu
    • To-go packaging or cups
    • Yard sign or door hanger
    • Tickets, passes, or wristbands
    • Venue signage and wayfinding
    • Event posters
  • Non-profit.
    • Fundraising or donation campaign
    • Event collateral (invite, program, banner)
    • Annual report spread
    • Donor thank-you or welcome kit
    • Impact infographic
    • Volunteer apparel
    • Supporter merch
    • Donor wall or environmental graphics
  • Any type.
    • Exterior signage
    • Merch
    • Interior or environmental graphics
    • Animated logo or motion piece

The Brand Guide

The guide documents your brand so someone else could use it correctly. Design it in your own brand system.

  • Intro.
    • Cover
    • Table of contents
  • Brand foundation.One or two pages, pulled from your deep-dive research:
    • The brand’s purpose
    • Who it’s for
    • What makes it different
    • Its personality
    • How it should be perceived, leading into the visuals
    • (The full research belongs in your Process Book.)
  • Logo.
    • Rationale
    • Primary logo
    • Lockups, with their grids
    • Clear space
    • Minimum size
    • Variations: positive, reversed, one color
    • Misuse examples
  • Color.
    • Every value: HEX, RGB, CMYK, Pantone
    • Each color’s role
  • Typography.
    • Exact typefaces and weights
    • Hierarchy
  • Visual elements.
    • Your style-scape
    • Supporting graphic elements
    • Photography direction
  • Applications.
    • Every application, shown in its mockup

What to Submit in Brightspace

  • One PDF: your Brand Guide.
    • Every application goes inside the guide. Don’t submit them as separate files.
    • Name it so I can tell what it is before I open it: your name and the brand.
  • Due Wednesday, December 9.
  • Separate assignments.
    • Your Process Book and Final Presentation go in their own Brightspace dropboxes, not this one.
